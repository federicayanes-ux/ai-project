01 / THE USEFUL BIT

What do we mean by AI?

The easiest example for me is a spam filter. It learns patterns that help it tell ordinary emails from junk. AI does that kind of thing on a much bigger scale: it takes information and produces predictions, recommendations or content. Chatbots are just one part of it. [1]

Generative AI uses patterns learned during training to produce things such as text and images. The bit I need to remember is that an answer can sound perfectly reasonable and still be wrong. [2]

Where I’d actually use it

A practice quiz seems like a sensible use. I could try the questions myself, then compare the answers with my notes. I can also see the appeal of a rough email draft when I’m staring at a blank page. I’d want to put it into my own words before sending it.

There are uses I wouldn’t have immediately thought of, too. The International Energy Agency describes AI helping predict renewable electricity generation and spot faults in power networks. That seems worth paying attention to alongside all the talk about chatbots. [3]

02 / THE CATCH

Would I trust the answer?

What worries me is how confident a wrong answer can sound. NIST lists risks such as made-up information, harmful bias and personal information being exposed. A neat paragraph might still contain an invented reference or repeat a stereotype. [2] So if I’m going to rely on something, I’d open the original source. And I wouldn’t put private information into a prompt just because it feels like a normal conversation.

THIS ONE WOULD MAKE ME PAUSE

“But it sounded like them.”

A familiar voice would make a request feel more believable to me. That’s why the US Federal Trade Commission’s warning about voice cloning stands out: scammers can copy a relative’s voice from a short audio clip. The advice is straightforward: call the person back on a number you already know. I’d rather make that extra call. [4]

Where would I draw the line?

I’d give it a go, then check the answers against my notes. A wrong quiz answer isn’t much help.

03 / THE BIGGER PICTURE

What’s happening behind the screen?

It’s easy to think of AI as something that just lives on a screen. But it runs on real equipment that needs electricity. The IEA’s 2025 report estimated that data centres used 415 TWh worldwide in 2024. It projected around 945 TWh in 2030, with AI driving the biggest share of the growth. Those numbers cover all data centres. [3]

The same report points to limits in electricity grids. [3] My takeaway is that this growth needs planning. I wouldn’t look at that forecast and jump straight to “AI will cause blackouts.”

There’s also the question of what happens when the services we depend on stop working. Cloudflare says a database-permissions change caused its widespread outage on 18 November 2025. [5] It makes me think I’d want a copy of my notes offline and some way to carry on without the service.

WHAT I’D LIKE TO SEE

I’d like AI to take some of the boring work off our hands and help us understand things we’re stuck on. But more capable tools could also make scams and cyberattacks easier. [2] For important decisions, I’d want a person checking what’s happening, clear limits on what the system can do, and a way to stop it. I’m interested in what AI can do. I’m also interested in what we might get used to letting it do for us.

A NOTE ABOUT THIS PROJECT

Why I made this page

I wanted this page to answer a question I keep coming back to: how much help from AI would I actually be comfortable with? That’s why I started with ordinary things like studying and writing an email, then looked at trust, electricity use and what happens when online services fail. The examples are situations I could imagine using AI in.

The notebook style fits the way I wanted to present it: a few explanations, a few doubts, and space to think. The page uses HTML, CSS and a small amount of JavaScript for the three choices above. The fonts are already on most devices, and the text can still be read with JavaScript turned off.

I used OpenAI Codex to help with the research, wording, illustration and code. The sources behind the factual claims are linked below. Where I’m giving my own take, I’ve tried to make that clear. Using AI to help make a page about AI felt like a good reason to be open about that help.
